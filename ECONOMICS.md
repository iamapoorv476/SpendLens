# ECONOMICS.md

## What A Converted Lead Is Worth To Credex

SpendLens is not valuable because of ad revenue or subscriptions. Its value comes from identifying technically sophisticated teams that are already operationally dependent on AI tooling and are likely candidates for:
- infrastructure financing,
- AI tooling procurement,
- usage credit management,
- or broader operational spend products from Credex.

The most realistic near-term monetization path is:
> high-intent operational leads.

A reasonable assumption:

- 1 qualified Credex customer generates:
  - ~$3,000–$10,000 yearly gross profit,
  - depending on financing volume, retained spend, or platform fees.

For modeling conservatively, assume:
- average annual gross profit per converted customer = **$4,000/year**

Now estimate lead conversion:

Out of 100 completed audits:
- ~20 submit email/contact
- ~6 book consultation
- ~2 become active Credex customers

That means:
- 1 customer per 50 completed audits.

If each customer is worth:
- $4,000/year,

then:
- each completed audit is economically worth:

> $4,000 ÷ 50 = ~$80 expected value per completed audit.

That is extremely strong economics for a lightweight audit workflow.

## CAC By Channel

### 1. Reddit / X Reply Mining
This is currently the strongest acquisition channel because users are already publicly discussing:
- Cursor pricing,
- Claude limits,
- workflow fragmentation,
- and AI spend frustration.

Expected CAC:
- Mostly time cost only.
- Estimated effective CAC:
  > $5–$20 per qualified audit completion.

This works because the user intent already exists.

---

### 2. Founder Communities / Indie Hackers
These users have lower scale but higher conversion quality because founders directly feel tooling costs.

Expected CAC:
> ~$20–$40 per qualified lead.

Lower volume, higher signal.

---

### 3. Organic Sharing Of Audit Results
If users begin sharing:
- “this tool found $500/mo waste”
- or:
- “this audit actually understood our workflow”

then distribution becomes partially self-propagating.

Expected CAC:
> Near $0 marginal cost.

This is the ideal long-term acquisition loop.

## What Conversion Rates Make This Profitable

Assume:
- average cost per completed audit = $15 blended CAC
- average customer value = $4,000/year gross profit

Now model funnel conversion:

### Funnel Assumptions
Out of 100 completed audits:
- 20 email captures (20%)
- 6 consultation bookings (30% of leads)
- 2 active customers (33% of consultations)

Result:
- 2 customers × $4,000 = $8,000 yearly gross profit

Acquisition cost:
- 100 audits × $15 CAC = $1,500

That creates:
> ~$6,500 gross contribution margin before operational costs.

Even if actual conversion rates are materially worse, the economics remain attractive because the users are already high-intent operational buyers.

## What Must Be True To Reach $1M ARR In 18 Months

To reach:
> $1M ARR

assuming:
- $4,000 average annual gross profit per customer,

Credex would need approximately:
- 250 active high-value customers.

Now reverse-engineer the funnel.

If:
- 2% of completed audits become customers,

then:
- 250 customers requires:
  > ~12,500 completed audits.

Over 18 months:
- ~694 completed audits/month
- ~23 completed audits/day

The 18-month path requires three things to be simultaneously true:
1. SpendLens becomes the default tool referenced in AI spend 
   discussions on Reddit and X — achievable through reply mining 
   and shareable audit results
2. Credex closes at least 33% of consultation calls — requires 
   the Credex sales conversation to feel like a natural extension 
   of the audit, not a separate pitch
3. Average customer value stays above $3,000/year — at risk if 
   Cursor, Anthropic, or OpenAI commoditizes pricing and reduces 
   the savings SpendLens can identify

## What Actually Determines Whether This Works

The critical risk is not traffic.

The real question is:
> do technical users trust the recommendations enough to continue into a financial relationship?

This is why:
- deterministic audit logic,
- transparent reasoning,
- workflow-aware recommendations,
- and avoiding “generic AI advice”

matter so much.

If users perceive the audit as:
- shallow,
- overly sales-driven,
- or operationally naive,

the funnel collapses immediately.

But if the audit consistently produces:
> “this tool actually understands how we work”

then the economics become unusually strong because the product sits directly inside an already-expanding operational spend category.
